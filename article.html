Study in Central Europe: Where You Can Study and Travel on Weekends

If you want university life and the chance to explore neighbouring countries, Central Europe can be an interesting place to start your research.

Campus Diaries•8 min read•2026
Quick idea: When comparing universities, don't only look at tuition or rankings. Look at the city, transport network, airport/train connections and your likely day-to-day budget too.

Studying abroad can be about more than the classroom. For students interested in exploring Europe, where you choose to study can shape how easy it is to take short trips during university breaks and weekends.

Why Central Europe?

Several cities in Central Europe sit within relatively easy reach of other major European destinations. Prague, Vienna, Budapest, Bratislava and cities in Poland are examples worth researching if travel is part of your university dream.

🇨🇿 Prague, Czechia

Prague is a major student and tourism centre with rail and bus connections to cities around the region. It can be a useful base for exploring nearby destinations, while still offering a large-city university environment.

Think about: university programmes, housing costs, transport connections and how much time you actually want to spend travelling during term.

🇦🇹 Vienna, Austria

Vienna is centrally positioned and has extensive rail connections. It can appeal to students who want a large international city and easy access to neighbouring countries. Costs can vary significantly depending on housing and lifestyle.

🇭🇺 Budapest, Hungary

Budapest is another major Central European city with an international student population and connections to surrounding countries. Compare current rent, transport, university fees and everyday costs.

🇵🇱 Poland

Poland has several large university cities, including Warsaw, Kraków and Wrocław. Each offers a different student experience and transport network, so compare individual cities rather than treating the whole country as one option.

🇸🇰 Bratislava, Slovakia

Bratislava is geographically close to other Central European capitals. Its smaller scale may appeal to students who prefer a less sprawling city while still wanting regional travel options.

How to compare cities before applying

  1. Start with the university. Check the exact course, language, accreditation and entry requirements.
  2. Check the real cost. Look at tuition, rent, food, transport, insurance and other required expenses.
  3. Map the connections. Search train, coach and airport routes from the city.
  4. Check travel rules. Visa, residence-permit and border rules depend on your nationality and circumstances and can change.
  5. Consider your routine. A city can be great for weekend trips, but you still need somewhere you will enjoy living and studying every day.
✦
The key takeaway
Choose your university for the education first, then use location and transport as part of the wider decision.

This is a starting point for research, not a visa or admissions guide. Always check current university, immigration and transport information from official sources before making plans.
