Study in Central Europe: Where You Can Study and Travel on Weekends

Imagine going to university in one European city and being able to take a weekend trip to another country without needing a huge travel budget or a long holiday. That's one of the biggest attractions of studying in Central Europe.

Central Europe is surrounded by countries with different cultures, languages, food and scenery. Depending on where you study, several major European destinations can be reached by train, bus or a short flight.

🇨🇿 Czechia — Prague

Prague is one of Europe's most recognizable student cities. Its central location makes it a useful base for exploring nearby countries.

Easy trips to consider: Germany, Austria, Slovakia and Poland.

Weekend idea: Spend a few days exploring Prague, then take a train to another Central European city during a university break.

🇦🇹 Austria — Vienna

Vienna combines a major European city with excellent connections to neighboring countries. Its location makes it particularly interesting for students who want to travel while studying.

Easy trips to consider: Slovakia, Czechia, Hungary and Slovenia.

Weekend idea: Bratislava is especially close, making it possible to combine two European capitals in one trip.

🇭🇺 Hungary — Budapest

Budapest is another major student destination with a central position in the region. It can be a convenient starting point for exploring Central and parts of Southeastern Europe.

Easy trips to consider: Austria, Slovakia, Czechia, Croatia and Slovenia.

Weekend idea: Spend a weekend in Vienna or Bratislava, then return to Budapest for classes on Monday.

🇵🇱 Poland — Kraków or Warsaw

Poland is a large country with several university cities. Kraków is particularly attractive for students who want a historic city with access to other destinations in the region.

Easy trips to consider: Czechia, Slovakia, Germany and Hungary.

🇸🇰 Slovakia — Bratislava

Bratislava has a particularly interesting location because it sits close to Austria and Hungary and is also within easy reach of Czechia.

Easy trips to consider: Austria, Hungary and Czechia.

For a student who wants to maximize the number of countries they can visit, its location is one of the biggest advantages.

🌍 How many countries could you visit?

It depends on your university location, budget, visa/residence status and how much free time you have. But Central Europe makes multi-country travel unusually practical because several borders are relatively close together.

For example, a student based in Vienna could potentially plan trips to Slovakia, Hungary, Czechia or Slovenia. A student based in Prague could look toward Germany, Austria, Poland or Slovakia.

🚆 Train or plane?

For nearby destinations, trains and long-distance buses can be much more convenient than flying because you avoid airport transfers and long check-in times. For longer journeys, compare both options before booking.

💸 How to keep student travel affordable

  • Plan trips around your university timetable rather than missing classes.
  • Compare trains, buses and flights instead of automatically choosing one.
  • Travel outside the busiest periods when possible.
  • Book accommodation early for popular weekends.
  • Travel with friends and split accommodation where appropriate.
  • Use student discounts whenever they are available.
  • Set a separate monthly travel budget.

🛂 Don't forget visas and residence rules

Before planning international trips, check the rules that apply to your passport, student residence permit and destination. Being able to travel easily between countries depends on your nationality and immigration status, so don't rely only on what another student tells you.

So, where should you study?

There isn't one perfect Central European city for everyone. Think about the university, tuition fees, living costs, language, student life and your travel goals together.

If travel is one of your biggest priorities, a university with strong transport connections can make exploring Europe much easier. But remember: you're choosing a university first and a travel base second.

✈️ The Campus Diaries idea

One of the best parts of studying abroad can be discovering that your university city is only the beginning. Your weekends, holidays and semester breaks can become opportunities to explore places you've only seen online.
