Study Abroad Essentials: The Ultimate University Moving Checklist

Moving to another country for university is exciting, but figuring out what to actually take with you can feel overwhelming. From important documents and money to shoes, luggage, technology and those little things that make a new room feel like home, here's a practical study abroad checklist to help you prepare.

1. Your Important Documents

This is the first thing to organize because replacing an important document while you're abroad can be much harder than replacing a pair of socks.

  • ☐ Passport
  • ☐ Student visa or residence permit
  • ☐ University acceptance letter
  • ☐ Enrollment or registration documents
  • ☐ Accommodation confirmation
  • ☐ Flight details
  • ☐ Health insurance documents
  • ☐ Academic transcripts
  • ☐ School certificates
  • ☐ Passport-size photographs
  • ☐ Emergency contact information
  • ☐ Digital copies of important documents

Keep your passport, visa documents, university paperwork and other important documents in your hand luggage rather than your checked suitcase.

3. Luggage

Your suitcase is going to be carrying a surprisingly large part of your life, so choose something practical rather than simply choosing the biggest suitcase you can find.

Consider the size and weight limits of your airline before buying luggage. A suitcase that looks enormous in your bedroom can become a problem when you have to lift it onto a train or pay an overweight baggage fee.

4. Shoes You'll Actually Wear

Shoes can take up a ridiculous amount of suitcase space, so think carefully before packing five pairs.

A comfortable everyday pair is usually more useful than another pair that only works with one outfit. Depending on where you're studying, you may also want weather-appropriate shoes, something slightly smarter for events and a pair for exercise.

  • ☐ Comfortable everyday sneakers
  • ☐ Weather-appropriate shoes
  • ☐ Smart shoes if needed
  • ☐ Sports shoes if you use them

5. Money & Banking

Before leaving, make sure you understand how you'll access your money in your new country.

  • ☐ Debit or credit card
  • ☐ Backup payment card
  • ☐ Some local currency
  • ☐ Emergency money
  • ☐ Banking app
  • ☐ Important bank information
  • ☐ First-month student budget

It's also worth checking your bank's international transaction and ATM fees before you travel.

6. Your Laptop & Technology

University life is going to involve a lot of technology, so make sure the essentials are packed somewhere you can easily access them.

  • ☐ Laptop
  • ☐ Laptop charger
  • ☐ Phone
  • ☐ Phone charger
  • ☐ Headphones
  • ☐ Power bank
  • ☐ USB drive if needed
  • ☐ Extension lead if appropriate

7. Clothes

The biggest packing mistake is usually bringing too many clothes.

Instead, check the climate of your university city and think about what you will realistically wear. Remember that you can buy things after you arrive.

  • ☐ Everyday outfits
  • ☐ Comfortable clothes
  • ☐ Underwear and socks
  • ☐ Sleepwear
  • ☐ Jacket or coat if needed
  • ☐ Rainwear if needed
  • ☐ Sportswear
  • ☐ One slightly formal outfit
  • ☐ Weather-appropriate clothing

8. Toiletries

You don't need to pack an entire bathroom. Bring enough of your essentials for your first few days and buy larger products after you arrive.

  • ☐ Toothbrush
  • ☐ Toothpaste
  • ☐ Shampoo
  • ☐ Shower products
  • ☐ Deodorant
  • ☐ Skincare
  • ☐ Hair products
  • ☐ Personal hygiene products

9. Medication & Health Essentials

If you take prescription medication, make sure you have enough for your trip and bring the relevant documentation. Check the rules of your destination country before travelling because medicines can be subject to different import rules.

  • ☐ Prescription medication
  • ☐ Prescription or medical documentation
  • ☐ Basic first-aid supplies
  • ☐ Health insurance information
  • ☐ Medical records if required
  • ☐ Glasses or contact-lens supplies if applicable

10. Your Student Room

Before buying room decorations and organization products, find out exactly what your accommodation already provides.

Some student residences provide bedding, kitchen equipment and storage, while others don't. Knowing this beforehand can save you from spending money and filling your suitcase with things you didn't need.

  • ☐ Bedsheets if not provided
  • ☐ Pillowcase
  • ☐ Towel
  • ☐ Laundry bag
  • ☐ Hangers
  • ☐ Small storage organizers
  • ☐ Desk organizers
  • ☐ A few personal decorations

11. Your Phone & SIM

Your phone is going to be one of your most useful tools during the first few days in a new country.

  • ☐ Make sure your phone is unlocked
  • ☐ Check international roaming
  • ☐ Research local SIM or eSIM options
  • ☐ Save emergency numbers
  • ☐ Save your accommodation address
  • ☐ Download offline maps
  • ☐ Download your university app if available

13. What Goes in Your Carry-On?

Your carry-on should contain the things you'd need if your checked luggage was delayed.

Keep these with you ✈️

  • Passport
  • Visa and residence documents
  • University documents
  • Wallet and cards
  • Phone
  • Laptop
  • Chargers
  • Medication
  • Important contact information
  • Accommodation details
  • One change of clothes
  • Basic toiletries

14. Things to Do Before You Leave

Packing is only half of preparing to study abroad. The week before you leave is a good time to make sure the practical side of your move is sorted.

  • ☐ Check your visa or residence requirements
  • ☐ Confirm your accommodation
  • ☐ Save your accommodation address
  • ☐ Research airport-to-accommodation transport
  • ☐ Research public transport
  • ☐ Find nearby grocery stores
  • ☐ Find a nearby pharmacy
  • ☐ Find the nearest medical facility
  • ☐ Save emergency numbers
  • ☐ Download useful maps
  • ☐ Check your university orientation information
  • ☐ Make a first-month budget
  • ☐ Tell family or friends your travel details

15. Things You Don't Need to Pack

This might be the most useful section of the entire checklist.

When you're moving abroad for the first time, it's easy to think you need to bring your entire life with you. You don't.

You probably don't need to pack huge amounts of shampoo, every book you own, ten pairs of shoes, kitchen equipment your accommodation already provides or decorations that you can easily buy later.

Think of your first trip as bringing the essentials that help you get settled. You can slowly build your new life once you're there.

16. The First-Week Essentials

Your first week will probably be chaotic in the best possible way. You'll be figuring out your university, your neighbourhood, public transport, groceries and where everything is.

Make your first week easier by having these ready:

  • ☐ Comfortable shoes
  • ☐ Everyday backpack
  • ☐ Phone with working connection
  • ☐ Bank card
  • ☐ Some local currency
  • ☐ Basic toiletries
  • ☐ Weather-appropriate clothes
  • ☐ University timetable or orientation information
  • ☐ Accommodation address

Final Thought

Moving to another country for university can feel like you're supposed to have everything figured out before you leave. You don't.

The goal isn't to pack your entire life into two suitcases. It's to bring the things that will help you arrive safely, settle in comfortably and start building your new routine.

Once you're there, you'll discover what you actually need — and that's part of the adventure.

Save this checklist before your study abroad move and come back to it while you're packing. 🌍✈️
