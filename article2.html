How to Compare European Student Cities Before You Apply

The university matters, but so does the city around it. Here is a simple checklist for comparing your options before making a study-abroad decision.

Quick idea: Don't compare cities using only one number, like tuition fees. Your real student experience is shaped by housing, transport, food, university quality, lifestyle and the practical rules that apply to you.

Choosing a city for university can feel overwhelming. There are dozens of European destinations, and every one seems to have a different combination of universities, prices, transport links and student culture.

The good news is that you don't need a complicated formula. A simple comparison checklist can help you narrow your choices and figure out which places deserve more research.

1. Start with the university, not Instagram

A beautiful city is not enough if the university does not offer the course, teaching language, accreditation or academic environment you need.

Start by checking the exact programme at each university. Look at entry requirements, application deadlines, tuition structure, course length and what is included in the programme.

Make a shortlist: Pick around 3–6 university programmes that genuinely fit what you want to study before comparing the cities around them.

2. Compare the real cost of living

Two cities can have similar tuition fees but very different everyday costs. Housing is often one of the biggest differences.

For each city, research monthly rent, utilities, groceries, public transport, phone plans and typical student expenses. If you plan to travel, create a separate travel budget rather than pretending it will cost nothing.

3. Look at housing before falling in love with a city

Student accommodation can vary from university residences to private rooms and shared apartments. Check how much accommodation actually costs, how competitive it is and how far it is from campus.

Also look at whether the advertised price includes utilities and what deposit or contract requirements apply.

4. Check public transport

A city with a good public transport network can make everyday life much easier. Check metro, tram, bus and train options, plus student discounts where available.

Ask yourself: Could I get to university easily? Could I reach the main train station or airport without spending a fortune? Would I need a car?

5. If travel matters, compare the connections

If one of your reasons for studying in Europe is exploring nearby countries, this deserves its own category.

Look at direct trains, long-distance coaches and flights from the city. A city can look geographically close to another destination but still be inconvenient to reach depending on the available routes.

✦
Travel tip
Compare the whole journey, not just the ticket price. A cheap ticket that requires several connections may be less useful for a short weekend than a slightly more expensive direct route.

6. Think about everyday student life

You are choosing somewhere to live, not just somewhere to visit. Research student organisations, libraries, sports, cafés, parks, cultural activities and the general atmosphere of the neighbourhoods where students live.

Think about what you personally enjoy. A huge capital city and a smaller university town can offer completely different experiences.

7. Check the rules that apply to you

Visa, residence-permit, healthcare and work rules depend on your nationality, destination and personal circumstances. These rules can also change.

Before applying or making financial commitments, use official government and university sources to check the requirements that apply to you. Don't rely on a social-media post or an old blog post for immigration information.

8. Make a simple comparison table

Once you have researched your options, put the information in one place. You can use a spreadsheet or even a notebook.

  1. University & course: Does the programme actually fit your goals?
  2. Tuition: What will you pay and when?
  3. Housing: What is a realistic monthly cost?
  4. Living expenses: Food, transport, phone and everyday spending.
  5. Transport: How easy is it to move around the city?
  6. Travel: What train, bus and flight connections are available?
  7. Student life: Would you enjoy living there for several years?
  8. Practical requirements: What visa, residence and university requirements apply?

My simple city-scorecard idea

You can give each category a personal rating from 1–5 just for your own comparison. The point is not to create a universal “best city”; it is to see which options match your priorities.

Example: If affordable housing matters most to you, give housing more weight. If weekend travel is a major priority, give transport and connections more weight. Your comparison should reflect the life you actually want.

Final checklist before choosing

Before you decide, make sure you have checked current information from the university and relevant official authorities. Prices, admission requirements, visa rules and transport options can change.

Most importantly, don't choose a city only because it looks amazing in photos. Choose a university and city combination that makes sense for your education, budget, lifestyle and plans.

This article is for general planning and does not replace official university, immigration or government guidance. Always verify current requirements for your nationality and destination before applying.
